# AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 — Access Openings
## Human Body Measurements — Principles for Determining Dimensions Required for Access Openings

---

## 3 General Requirements

Operations requiring reach through minimum access openings are likely to be less efficient, less safe and less healthy than working with unrestricted access. Therefore, before installing access openings other options should be considered, e.g. possibility to open machinery, withdrawal of parts for repair. This is particularly important where the task demands frequent access.

When access openings cannot be avoided the following criteria are of particular significance:

**a) Ease of access is influenced by:**
- The demands of the task, e.g. posture, nature and speed of movement, lines of sight, application of force
- The location of the access opening relative to the position of the person, e.g. convenient height above floor, within easy reach, sufficient space outside to allow adoption of a comfortable posture, sufficient space inside to allow performance of the task
- Frequency and duration of task
- Whether tools are being carried, e.g. for maintenance or repair purposes
- Length of access openings e.g. through a relatively thin wall or through a channel type opening
- Whether additional equipment such as PPE (including protective clothing) or portable lighting is being carried or worn
- The type of clothing, e.g. light or heavy clothing, bare hands or thick gloves, bare headed or wearing a helmet

**b)** Environmental conditions (e.g. darkness, heat, noise, moisture)

**c)** Level of risk during the task

> Therefore, in addition to the anthropometric data, allowances shall be provided for the respective opening dimensions and reach distances, taking into account the above criteria.

---

## 4 Access Openings

An access opening is an opening through which a person can lean forward, reach forward, or extend the upper body, head, arm, hand, a finger or several fingers, leg or foot, to be able to carry out measures during work procedures.

This standard specifies **minimum** dimensions. Wherever possible, basic dimensions for openings should be increased, and maximum dimensions for reach should be decreased.

> **Note:** P95 = 95th percentile of the expected user population

## Annex A (Normative) — Application of Measurements in Practice

### A.0 Introduction

The opening dimensions including allowances in this standard do not always take into consideration:
- Aspects of health and safety arising from contact with the access opening itself
- Body positions and movements that may risk the user's safety and health
- Whether the person has to adopt a certain body position to meet force demands of the task
- Space required for transportation of equipment and tools through the access opening
- Space required for using equipment and tools in an ergonomic way (cleaning, repair, maintenance)
- Personal protective equipment the user might wear
- Speed reduction caused by a too narrow space
- Special visual demands on the user
- Any mental load factor (e.g. task must be completed within a specific period of time)
- Space requirements for entrance to and exit from the access opening

### A.1 Principles for Determining Additional Space

For each opening a number of allowances are described for conditions that need to be taken into account when determining the practical size. These allowances shall be added to the anthropometric measurements to ensure safety and health. Allowances are not simply additive — some conditions overlap. An expert should determine which conditions are applicable and the most critical.

## Annex B (Informative) — Position of Access Openings

### B.1 Purpose
This annex gives information on the position of access openings to facilitate access for the intended user population.

### B.2 Provision for Adjustability
In some cases, access can only be ensured by provision of a supporting surface that can be changed in height to accommodate people of differing anthropometric dimensions. The height of the symbol (Figure B.1) represents the difference in stature between the 5th percentile (small person) and the 95th percentile (tall person).

> For allowances for footwear, clothing etc. see Annex A.

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figure B.1 — adjustability symbol

### B.3 Conditions to be Met in Location of Access Openings

> **Note:** In all Annex B figures, all dimensions are in mm.

#### B.3.1 Access Opening for the Upper Body and Arms (see 4.1)

- Sufficient space should be allowed underneath the access opening (min ≥1220 mm)
- For access openings of minimum size per clause 4.1, this should accommodate a tall person crouching
- Increasing opening size allows space underneath to be reduced but not less than given in clause 4.5 of EN 547-1
- For monitoring tasks, height of access opening above supporting surface should be ≤ shoulder height of a small person standing upright
- If task requires use of arms, opening should be below the elbow of the person when standing upright

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figures B.2, B.3, B.4, B.5 for positioning diagrams

#### B.3.2 Access Opening for the Head as Far as the Shoulders — Inspection Tasks (see 4.2)

- Sufficient space should be allowed on the inside to accommodate the head to the shoulders (≥320 mm clearance, opening height ≥1560–1240 mm range)
- When in a vertical surface, persons can only look forward, downward and sideways — provision for a carefully positioned supporting surface and hand holds is essential
- Work should be only of short duration
- Entry into an access opening for the head may cause distress — for frequent access, other means are recommended (e.g. video monitoring)

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figures B.6, B.7 for positioning diagrams

#### B.3.3 Access Opening for Both Arms (Either Forward or Downward) (see 4.3)

- For vertical surfaces, dimensions apply only to access openings located between shoulder and elbow height with body in upright posture (≥500 mm, 1500–1350 mm range)
- Posture can only be maintained if supporting surface height can be adjusted (platforms, steps)
- Where adjustments are not provided, dimensions must be increased or reach distance reduced
- Required field of vision should be maintained e.g. by providing windows
- For crouching posture without knee space, effective operating arm length is reduced by 30% — such use should be only infrequent and short duration
- For horizontal surface downward entry, space should be allowed outside openings for operator's body
- If distance from opening to farthest point is greater than t₁, increase dimensions to allow entry of upper body

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figures B.8, B.9, B.10 for positioning diagrams

#### B.3.4 Access Opening for Both Lower Arms up to the Elbow, Either Forward or Downward (see 4.4)

- For vertical surfaces, dimensions apply only to access openings between shoulder and elbow height with body in upright posture (1560–1240 mm, 1240–320 mm range)
- Posture can only be maintained if supporting surface height can be changed (seats, platforms, steps)
- Where changes are not possible, dimensions must be increased and/or reach distance reduced
- Required field of vision should be maintained e.g. by providing windows
- Only where access is infrequent and short duration, opening may be located between shoulder height of a small person standing upright and elbow height of a tall person crouching (≥1220 mm, ≥500 mm)

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figures B.11, B.12 for positioning diagrams

#### B.3.5 Opening for Access to the Side for One Arm up to the Shoulder Joint (see 4.5)

- For vertical surfaces, dimensions apply only to access openings located at shoulder height with body in upright posture (1560–1300 mm, ≥320 mm)
- Posture can only be maintained if supporting surface height can be changed (platforms, steps)
- Where changes are not possible, dimension A should be increased and/or reach distance B decreased
- Required field of vision should be maintained e.g. by providing windows
- Only where access is infrequent and short duration, opening may be located between shoulder height of a small person standing upright and shoulder height of a tall person crouching (≥1220 mm, ≥902 mm)

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figures B.13, B.14 for positioning diagrams

#### B.3.6 Access Opening for One Lower Arm up to the Elbow (see 4.6)

- For vertical surfaces, dimensions apply only to access openings between shoulder and elbow height with body in upright posture (1200–1560 mm range)
- Posture can only be maintained if supporting surface height can be changed (platforms, steps)
- Where changes are not possible, dimension A should be increased and/or reach distance B decreased
- Required field of vision should be maintained e.g. by providing windows
- Only where access is infrequent and short duration, opening may be located between body height of a small person standing upright and elbow height of a tall person crouching (≤1120 mm, ≥600 mm)

> 📐 See AS/NZS 4024.1703:2014 Figures B.15, B.16 for positioning diagrams

#### B.3.7 Access Opening for Fist (see 4.7)

- Access opening should be within easy reach of the operator with body in upright posture
- For infrequent and short duration use, operator need not maintain upright posture and opening may be placed farther away as long as it remains within reach
- For further information see B.3.6

## Annex C (Informative) — Notations for Dimensions and Anthropometric Body Measurements

### Notation System

| Letter | General Meaning |
|---|---|
| h | Height of whole body or part of body |
| a | Breadth of trunk including arms and shoulders; breadth of body part |
| b | Depth of body or body part; in one case used for forward reach |
| c | Length of body part or segment |
| d | Diameter of body part having approximately circular cross section |
| t | Functional reach or extension of body part |
| x, y | Allowances |

> Indices (subscripts) are used in order. When a measure is taken in both standing and sitting, the standing index is a one digit number; the sitting index is 10 higher.
> When a specific percentile is referred to, it is denoted by the actual percent figure preceded by 'P' within brackets after the index (e.g. P95, P5).
